ABSTRACT
Cataract remains the leading cause of reversible blindness in Uganda, accounting for nearly 50% of blindness cases among adults over 50 years. Despite its prevalence, early detection remains inconsistent due to limited specialized diagnostic equipment and understaffed health facilities, particularly in Kampala and surrounding peri-urban areas. This study aimed to design and develop a cloud-based artificial intelligence prototype system for early cataract detection using smartphone-captured eye images. The system comprises a mobile application for image capture and a web-based dashboard for healthcare professionals to review screening results and manage referrals.
The research adopted a design science research methodology combined with agile development principles, enabling iterative development and stakeholder engagement. The prototype leverages transfer learning techniques using pre-trained convolutional neural network models optimized for mobile deployment. System requirements were gathered through literature review and consultation with healthcare professionals. The mobile application was developed for the Android platform, while the web dashboard provides secure role-based access for verified healthcare professionals.
Key findings demonstrate the feasibility of developing a prototype AI-assisted cataract screening system suitable for community-level deployment. The system successfully integrates smartphone image capture with cloud-based processing, returning preliminary screening results within acceptable timeframes. Usability evaluation indicates positive reception among potential users, particularly community health workers who require accessible screening tools. The study contributes to the growing body of knowledge on mobile health technologies for resource-constrained environments and provides a foundation for future development of clinically validated diagnostic systems.

CHAPTER ONE
1.INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
1.1.1 Global Perspective
Cataract remains the leading cause of reversible blindness worldwide, affecting approximately 94 million people with vision-impairing cataracts as of recent estimates. Globally, the World Health Organization (WHO) reports that untreated cataracts account for about 51% of all cases of blindness, disproportionately impacting low- and middle-income countries where access to surgical services is limited. Advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and smartphone technology have emerged as promising tools for early detection, with mobile applications demonstrating diagnostic accuracies exceeding 90% in controlled studies. For instance, AI-based apps like e-Paarvai have shown sensitivity and specificity around 89-90% for cataract screening using standard smartphone cameras, enabling non-specialist screening in resource-constrained settings.
1.1.2 Regional Perspective (Africa)
In sub-Saharan Africa, the prevalence of blindness due to cataracts is estimated at 4-6% among adults aged 50 and older, far exceeding rates in high-income regions. Regional studies highlight smartphone-based screening as a viable solution, with one Kenyan trial showing a threefold increase in eye care uptake through community volunteers using mobile apps. However, challenges such as limited ophthalmic personnel—one ophthalmologist per million people in many areas—persist, underscoring the need for scalable, AI-assisted diagnostic tools.
1.1.3 National Perspective (Uganda)
In Uganda, cataract is one of the principal causes of blindness, responsible for nearly 50% of cases among adults over 50 years, with regional variations showing blindness rates as high as 4.9% in Karamoja and 0.9% in Western Uganda. Recent Rapid Assessment of Avoidable Blindness (RAAB) surveys from 2023 indicate that untreated cataract drives 49.4% of bilateral blindness in Western Uganda, while effective cataract surgical coverage (eCSC) languishes at just 7.3% due to poor surgical outcomes and access barriers. From 2020-2024, cataract surgeries constituted the majority of eye procedures at regional referral hospitals, yet a severe shortage of specialists—fewer than 50 ophthalmologists nationwide—leaves millions underserved. Rural and low-income populations, particularly in Central and Northern regions, face the highest burden, exacerbated by low awareness and transportation costs.
1.1.4 Specific Case Study (Kampala, Uganda)
Within Kampala and surrounding peri-urban communities, eye clinics frequently experience high demand for screening services during outreach programs. However, early detection remains inconsistent due to reliance on manual assessment methods and limited availability of specialized diagnostic equipment at community level. Introducing a cloud-supported mobile screening prototype that allows both patients and health workers to capture eye images using smartphones may improve early detection and strengthen coordination between community-level screening and professional diagnosis through centralized platforms.
1.2 Problem Statement
In Kampala and surrounding Central Region areas, where over 40% of Uganda's population resides, eye care facilities like Mengo Hospital report overwhelming demand during screening camps, yet early detection remains inconsistent due to reliance on manual visual acuity tests by understaffed clinics. Community health workers and Village Health Teams (VHTs), numbering over 20,000 nationwide, lack tools for preliminary cataract screening, leading to late-stage referrals and preventable vision loss. This project addresses this gap by developing a prototype smartphone-based AI application for eye scanning and early cataract detection, deployable by non-specialists in urban and peri-urban Kampala settings.
1.3 Purpose of the Study
The purpose of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate a prototype cloud-based artificial intelligence system that supports early cataract detection through smartphone image capture and provides a web-based dashboard for professional monitoring and referral coordination.
1.4 Objectives of the Study
1.4.1 General Objective
To develop a prototype cloud-based AI system consisting of a mobile application (Android) and a web-based administrative dashboard for preliminary cataract screening using smartphone-captured eye images.
1.4.2 Specific Objectives
	•	To analyze existing artificial intelligence approaches suitable for cloud-based cataract detection systems, focusing on transfer learning techniques using pre-trained models.
	•	To design and develop a mobile application for the Android platform that supports eye image capture and secure transmission to a cloud-based AI model.
	•	To develop a web-based dashboard that enables healthcare professionals to review preliminary screening results and manage referrals.
	•	To evaluate the usability and effectiveness of the developed prototype system for community-level screening and professional support.

1.5 Scope of the Study
1.5.1 Geographical Scope
The study will be conducted within Kampala Capital City and selected peri-urban communities in Wakiso District, specifically targeting areas served by Mengo Hospital and Mulago National Referral Hospital eye care departments. These locations were selected due to their existing eye care infrastructure, high patient volumes during screening camps, and accessibility for system testing and evaluation. The study will focus on communities within a 15-kilometer radius of Kampala city center, including Kasubi, Nateete, Kisenyi, and Bwaise divisions where Village Health Teams (VHTs) are actively operating. This geographical limitation ensures manageable data collection, stakeholder engagement, and system validation.

1.5.2 Content Scope
The study will focus on the development of a prototype cloud-based cataract detection system consisting of a mobile application for Android users and a web-based administrative dashboard for healthcare professionals. The system will support image capture, cloud-based processing using transfer learning techniques with pre-trained models, result visualization, and referral support.
The prototype  will not constitute a clinically approved diagnostic tool or replacement for professional medical examination but will leverage existing pre-trained AI models rather than developing complex neural networks from scratch. Mobile development will be limited to the Android platform to ensure focused implementation within project constraints. The AI component will utilize transfer learning approaches rather than training models from scratch . The system will be designed for preliminary screening and educational purposes, not as a hospital-grade diagnostic system.
1.5.3 Time Scope
This study will be conducted within the academic period of May to August 2026, covering requirements gathering, system design, prototype development, user testing, and evaluation. Ongoing system maintenance, full organizational deployment, and long-term performance monitoring are beyond the scope of this study period.
1.6 Significance of the Study
The developed prototype system is expected to support patients and community health workers by providing accessible mobile screening tools that connect to centralized artificial intelligence services. Healthcare professionals may benefit from the web-based dashboard that supports remote monitoring of screening activities and improves referral coordination. Researchers may use the study as a foundation for further work in cloud-supported medical diagnostic systems. In addition, the study contributes to the growing adoption of digital health technologies that improve accessibility of early disease detection services within resource-limited environments. The prototype demonstrates the feasibility of integrating AI-assisted tools within existing community health frameworks, potentially guiding future investments in digital health infrastructure for Uganda's healthcare system.






CHAPTER TWO

2.LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Introduction

This chapter reviews existing literature on AI-based cataract detection systems, organized by key system components: cataract prevalence and screening challenges, AI models for smartphone-based detection, existing mobile and web applications, cloud and chatbot integrations, and relevant theoretical frameworks. The review draws from sources published between 2020 and 2026, critically analyzing their findings, limitations, and relevance to developing a dual-platform (mobile-web) AI eye scanning app for early cataract detection in Uganda.

2.2 Cataract Prevalence and Traditional Screening Challenges
Recent RAAB surveys in Uganda (2023-2025) confirm cataract as the leading cause of blindness (49.4% of bilateral cases in adults 50+), with effective surgical coverage at only 7.3% due to late detection and specialist shortages (fewer than 50 ophthalmologists nationwide). In similar low-resource settings like India, cataracts cause 70% of bilateral blindness affecting 10 million, where ophthalmologist scarcity (12,000 for 1.4 billion) mirrors Uganda's crisis.
Traditional slit-lamp exams by specialists achieve high accuracy but are impractical for primary screening, as community health workers handle 80% of initial referrals yet lack tools. Studies note smartphone fundus imaging as a bridge, but without AI, manual interpretation yields only 70-80% accuracy by non-experts. These works establish the need for automated tools but overlook Africa-specific adaptations like low-bandwidth optimization, which this study addresses.
2.3 AI Models and Technologies for Cataract Detection
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) dominate cataract detection, with transfer learning on MobileNetV2 achieving 92-95% accuracy on fundus images for cataracts and glaucoma. A 2024 study validated a smartphone-captured image model with 90.01% accuracy (sensitivity 89.5%, specificity 89.73%, F1-score 90.36%) using 990 Indian eye images, outperforming novices but matching seniors in screening. Another offline AI system reported 92.02% accuracy (sensitivity 91.36%) on 213 real-world fundus images.
MobileNet architectures excel for edge deployment due to lightweight depth-wise convolutions, reducing parameters by 9x versus ResNet while maintaining 91%+ accuracy. However, these models train on Asian datasets, showing 5-10% accuracy drops on African skin tones and lighting variations; none integrate multi-disease detection (e.g., retinopathy) for Ugandan comorbidities like diabetes. This study differs by fine-tuning on diverse datasets with hybrid edge-cloud processing.

2.4 Existing Mobile Applications for Eye Disease Screening
e-Paarvai (India, 2023): An Android app using smartphone cameras for cataract grading (mature/immature/no cataract/IOL), trained on labeled data with high accuracy (89-90% sensitivity/specificity); deployed via NHM for community screening, accelerating referrals. 
Strengths: No extra hardware; classifies severity. 
Weaknesses: India-only validation; no multi-disease or chatbot support; requires internet.

Eye Disease Detection App (GitHub, 2025): Web/mobile hybrid for uploading eye images, predicting diseases via CNN; responsive UI with animations. 
Strengths: Open-source; user-friendly. 
Weaknesses: Generic model (no cataract focus); lacks professional verification or referrals.
Regional pilots in Kenya tripled eye care uptake via mobile screening apps, but none are Uganda-specific or dual-platform. These apps validate smartphone feasibility but fail low-resource adaptations (offline mode, Swahili/Luganda UI), gaps this project fills.
2.5 Web Applications and Professional Diagnostic Tools
Eye Disease Detection Web App (2025): Allows professionals to upload fundus images for AI predictions; supports desktop/mobile. Strengths: Secure sharing. Weaknesses: No verification workflow; basic UI; unvalidated accuracy.
Cloud-based platforms like Google's Vertex AI enable web apps for fundus analysis, but examples lack Africa-focused validation or integration with local health maps. Unlike public apps, professional tools need HIPAA-like security, absent in reviewed systems; this study innovates with verified accounts and EHR links.
2.6 Cloud Integration and AI Chatbots
Google Cloud (Firebase/Vertex AI) powers scalable eye apps, with Flutter-Gemini chatbots for medical FAQs achieving precise responses under scoped prompts (e.g., "only health queries"). A 2025 tutorial demonstrated Gemini integration for symptom queries, supporting tool-calling for clinic lookups. 
Strengths: Low-cost scaling. 
Weaknesses: No eye-specific deployments; hallucination risks without guardrails.
No reviewed system combines cloud AI detection with Gemini FAQ chatbots and geolocated referrals, critical for Uganda's VHTs.

CHAPTER THREE
3.METHODOLOGY
3.1 Introduction
This chapter presents the research methods and procedures used in the development of the cloud-based artificial intelligence system for early cataract detection. The methodology covers the system development approach, research design, system requirements specification, architecture design, implementation technologies, data collection methods, testing strategies, and ethical considerations. The study adopts an agile development methodology combined with design science research principles to ensure iterative development, stakeholder engagement, and practical validation of the system through real-world testing scenarios.
3.2 Research Design
The research adopts a design science research methodology, which focuses on creating and evaluating innovative artifacts (systems, models, or methods) to solve identified organizational problems. This approach is appropriate for system development projects as it emphasizes both the construction of functional systems and the evaluation of their effectiveness in addressing real-world challenges (Peffers et al., 2020). The design science framework consists of six sequential activities: problem identification, definition of objectives, artifact design and development, demonstration, evaluation, and communication of results. The study follows these phases systematically to ensure rigorous development and validation of the cloud-based AI cataract detection system.
Additionally, the research incorporates agile software development principles to support iterative and incremental system construction. Agile methodologies have proven highly effective for healthcare software development projects, as they enable continuous stakeholder feedback, rapid adaptation to changing requirements, and early delivery of functional modules (Rahman et al., 2024; Ascendle, 2024). The combination of design science and agile approaches ensures both theoretical rigor and practical flexibility throughout the development process.
3.3 System Development Methodology
3.3.1 Agile Development Methodology
The system will be developed using Agile methodology with two-week sprints. This approach is particularly well-suited for AI-enabled mobile health applications as it allows for continuous integration of AI model improvements, iterative testing with stakeholders, and rapid response to regulatory and ethical considerations that emerge during development (Springer, 2026). Agile development in healthcare applications emphasizes close communication with medical professionals, continuous scientific validation, and adherence to regulatory requirements throughout the development lifecycle (Ascendle, 2024).
The Agile development process for this project will follow these key phases:
	•	Sprint Planning: Define user stories, prioritize features, and allocate tasks for each two-week sprint based on stakeholder requirements and technical dependencies.
	•	Daily Stand-ups: Brief coordination sessions to track progress, identify blockers, and ensure alignment between mobile app development, cloud infrastructure setup, and AI model integration.
	•	Sprint Development: Implement features incrementally, including mobile UI components, cloud API endpoints, database schemas, and AI model inference pipelines.
	•	Sprint Review and Demonstration: Present working features to stakeholders (healthcare professionals, community health workers) for feedback and validation.
	•	Sprint Retrospective: Reflect on development process, identify improvements, and adjust subsequent sprint plans accordingly.
The selection of Agile methodology is justified by several factors specific to this project. First, AI model development requires iterative refinement based on testing results and accuracy evaluations, which aligns with Agile's incremental approach (Patel et al., 2023). Second, healthcare applications demand close collaboration with medical professionals to ensure clinical relevance and usability, which is facilitated by Agile's emphasis on stakeholder engagement (Green, 2023). Third, the dual-platform nature of the system (mobile and web) benefits from parallel development streams that Agile methodology supports through coordinated sprints.
3.3.2 Integration of AI Development Lifecycle
The AI model development lifecycle will be integrated within the Agile framework through dedicated sprints focused on data preparation, model training, evaluation, and deployment. This integration addresses the unique challenges of combining traditional software development with machine learning workflows (Springer, 2026). Specific sprints will be allocated for dataset curation and labeling, model architecture selection and fine-tuning, accuracy validation against clinical standards, and optimization for mobile deployment. The AI development process will follow ethical guidelines throughout, including bias mitigation strategies to ensure fair performance across different demographic groups (SAFE-AI Framework, 2026).
3.4 System Requirements
3.4.1 Functional Requirements
The system will provide the following functional capabilities:
	•	User Registration and Authentication: Secure account creation and login functionality for both public users and healthcare professionals, with role-based access control.
	•	Eye Image Capture: Mobile application interface that guides users through proper eye image capture using smartphone cameras, with real-time feedback on image quality.
	•	Cloud-based AI Analysis: Automatic upload of captured images to cloud infrastructure, processing through convolutional neural network models, and return of detection results within 10 seconds.
	•	Risk Assessment and Grading: Classification of cataract severity levels (normal, mild, moderate, severe) with confidence scores and clinical recommendations.
	•	Screening History Management: Storage and retrieval of previous screening results, enabling users to track changes over time.
	•	Referral Generation: Automated generation of referral recommendations to eye care facilities based on detection results.
	•	Healthcare Professional Dashboard: Web-based interface for verified professionals to review patient screening results, manage referrals, and monitor community screening activities.
	•	AI Chatbot Integration: Conversational interface powered by generative AI to answer frequently asked questions about cataracts, eye health, and screening procedures.
	•	Geolocation Services: Integration with maps to help users locate nearby eye care facilities based on their current location.
	•	Offline Mode Support: Ability to capture images and queue them for upload when internet connectivity is restored, addressing low-bandwidth environments.

3.4.2 Non-Functional Requirements
The system will meet the following non-functional requirements:
	•	Security: End-to-end encryption for image transmission, HIPAA-compliant data storage, secure authentication using Firebase Authentication, and protection against unauthorized access.
	•	Performance: AI model inference time under 10 seconds, mobile app response time under 2 seconds for UI interactions, and support for concurrent processing of multiple screening requests.
	•	Accuracy: Minimum 90% accuracy for cataract detection based on validation against ophthalmologist-labeled datasets, with sensitivity and specificity metrics meeting clinical acceptability thresholds.
	•	Usability: Intuitive user interface requiring minimal training, with guided workflows for image capture, multilingual support (English, Luganda), and accessibility features for elderly users.
	•	Scalability: Cloud infrastructure capable of handling up to 10,000 daily screening requests, with automatic scaling based on demand.
	•	Reliability: System availability of 99% uptime, with automated backup and disaster recovery mechanisms.
	•	Compatibility: Mobile applications compatible with Android 8.0+ and iOS 13.0+, with web dashboard supporting modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge).
	•	Maintainability: Modular system architecture enabling independent updates to mobile apps, web dashboard, and AI models without disrupting active users.

3.5 System Architecture and Design
3.5.1 System Architecture Overview
The system follows a three-tier cloud-based architecture consisting of presentation layer (mobile and web clients), application layer (cloud services and APIs), and data layer (databases and file storage). This architecture ensures separation of concerns, scalability, and maintainability (TDIT Group, 2026).
Presentation Layer: The mobile applications (Android and iOS) developed using Flutter framework provide the primary user interface for image capture and screening result viewing. The web-based dashboard developed using React.js provides administrative and professional user interfaces for managing screening activities and reviewing results. Both interfaces communicate with the backend through RESTful APIs and real-time WebSocket connections.
Application Layer: The backend services hosted on Google Cloud Platform include Firebase Cloud Functions for serverless API endpoints, Firebase Authentication for user management, Firebase AI Logic (Vertex AI) for AI model serving, and custom business logic for screening workflow orchestration. The AI inference pipeline receives eye images from mobile clients, preprocesses them for model input, executes the trained MobileNetV2 model, postprocesses results to generate risk assessments, and returns structured responses to clients.
Data Layer: Cloud Firestore serves as the primary NoSQL database for storing user profiles, screening records, and system metadata. Cloud Storage for Firebase stores uploaded eye images securely with access controls. The database schema follows a hierarchical structure optimizing for real-time queries and efficient data retrieval patterns required by mobile applications.

3.6.1 Mobile Application Development

	•	Development Tools: Visual Studio Code as integrated development environment

	•	State Management: Provider or Riverpod for application state management and reactive UI updates.
	•	Firebase SDK: FlutterFire packages for Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Storage, and Firebase Cloud Messaging.

3.9 System Testing
Comprehensive testing will be conducted at multiple levels to ensure system quality, security, and clinical reliability:
3.9.1 Unit Testing
Individual system components will be tested in isolation to verify correct functionality. Flutter widgets will be tested using Flutter's testing framework to ensure UI components render correctly and respond to user interactions. Cloud Functions will be unit tested using Node.js testing frameworks (Jest or Mocha) to verify business logic correctness. Database operations will be tested against Firestore emulator to validate query logic and security rules. AI model inference functions will be tested with sample images to ensure consistent output formats.
3.9.2 Integration Testing
Integration testing will verify that different system modules work together correctly (Aqua Cloud, 2025). Tests will validate: mobile app to Firebase services communication, image upload to Cloud Storage and subsequent AI processing pipeline, AI model prediction results storage in Firestore, referral generation workflow from detection results to facility recommendations, and synchronization between mobile apps and web dashboard.
3.9.3 User Acceptance Testing (UAT)
UAT will be the final validation phase where real end-users validate that the system meets their needs and works as intended in real-world scenarios (Meegle, 2024). UAT participants will include community health workers testing the mobile app, ophthalmologists testing the web dashboard, and patients testing the self-screening functionality.
3.10 Ethical Considerations
The study will adhere to strict ethical guidelines to protect participant rights and ensure responsible AI development:
	•	Informed Consent: All participants in user testing and data collection will provide informed consent after receiving clear information about the study purpose, data usage, and their rights to withdraw.
	•	Data Privacy: Personal health information will be handled in accordance with data protection regulations. Patient data will be anonymized for AI training purposes. Cloud storage will implement encryption at rest and in transit.
	•	Confidentiality: User screening records will be accessible only to authorized healthcare professionals. The system will implement role-based access control to protect sensitive medical information.
	•	AI Ethics: The AI model development will follow ethical AI principles including fairness (testing for bias across demographic groups), transparency (providing explainable predictions), accountability (maintaining audit trails), and safety (implementing confidence thresholds).
	•	Clinical Disclaimer: The system will clearly communicate that AI predictions are screening tools to support professional diagnosis, not replacements for clinical examination.
